Applies when there is no NOTES.md for the topic yet, or when NOTES.md says `stage: start`. This stage takes the author's topic to an approved structure, ledger and set of modes.

1. **Ask what you cannot find.** Take the topic from the author's message. In one numbered list, ask only what you cannot find yourself: the topic, as the first question, when the message gives none; where the sources are (older versions, notes, the material the document describes); who reads the document and what they do with it; and where the document lives, as one Markdown file or a folder of them. Skip any of these that the author's message or the sources already answer. These are framing questions only: questions about the content, such as how something works or who does it, wait for the piece that covers it, where the structure gives them context and the mode decides who answers them. In the same list, invite the author to paste any context they have, in any form, because a loose dump costs them less than answering questions one by one. When nothing is left to ask, go straight to step 2.

   If you have questions: **Stop.** Create NOTES.md from references/notes-template.md, with `stage: start`, the topic when you have it and `waiting_for: "answers to the start questions"`. Until step 4 settles the working folder, the current folder stands in for it, and the rule in SKILL.md under Working files about an existing NOTES.md applies. Then ask, and end the turn with the menu.

   Save any context the author pastes at the start, in the first message or in an answer, verbatim to inputs/start.md before you work with it, because the finish stage checks the document against it.

2. **Read the sources.** For many or large sources, if you can run subagents, give each source to one and have it return a short summary with file and line references; otherwise read them one after another. List each source under Sources in NOTES.md with a line on what it covers. The sources are background: in author-led pieces the author's view wins over them, and you name each conflict you find, because the author decides what the document says and needs the conflict in view to decide.

3. **Propose two or three structures.** Mark one as recommended, with a one-line reason. Each structure states:
   - the reader, and the one purpose of the document;
   - the pieces in order, each with a title, its file and one line on its content, and, for a piece that describes part of a process, its input and output;
   - which pieces get a diagram: only those where a picture explains a flow, a structure or a comparison better than text does;
   - whether the document opens with a summary, and in which file, because the finish stage writes it last;
   - when the document describes decisions its readers make or wait for, where the reader finds who decides: a sentence at each decision, and for many decisions also a table in a piece or section of its own (human-writing's rule "When the reader acts on decisions");
   - what is left out on purpose.

   Apply "Decide what the reader needs" from the human-writing skill to each candidate. A long document shows the whole before the parts, in the form that fits it (human-writing's rule "When the document is long"). When the document describes a process, that is usually a first piece with an overview diagram of its steps, with no roles, tool names or technical detail, and each box of it becomes one later piece. The structure gets its own stop because it shapes the document more than any later edit, and alternatives side by side show the author choices they would not have thought to ask for.

   **Stop.** Write the proposed structures in full to NOTES.md under Questions, and set `stage: start`, the topic, the sources, an empty pieces list and `waiting_for: "choice of structure"`. Write nothing into the document yet, because no content is approved yet. Ask the author to pick, merge or edit a structure, and end the turn with the menu.

4. **Propose the working setup** in one message, once the structure is approved:
   - The concept ledger: what the reader already knows, and the terms the document will introduce, with a budget that fits the reader and the document's size and that also covers any roles below.
   - When the document describes decisions its readers make or wait for, an owner role for each decision. List the decisions, and propose for each the role that owns it, taking the role names from the sources, the author or the reader's field: for example, the duty nurse or the attending physician in a clinic, or the product owner or the tech lead in a software team. Give each role one line on what it decides, for the Roles table of the ledger, and mark the roles you propose as defaults that each organization maps to its own people. A reader who knows which role decides can find the person who holds it, while "a person approves" leaves them asking who. The author confirms or edits the roles, and may instead choose "a person approves" as a standing rule.
   - A mode for each piece with a one-line reason, and the default mode. As a rule of thumb, descriptive pieces that the sources fully cover (reference pages, descriptions of forms or tools, examples) are auto; pieces that state decisions, scope, recommendations or how the organization works are author-led; the rest are step-by-step.
   - Standing rules to confirm, such as "US spelling" or "no product names in headings", and any names that must not appear. Turn each rule that a pattern can detect reliably into a check: a regex with its reason. The other rules stay standing rules only, and the consistency lens checks them at the review.
   - The working folder, and how the author will preview drafts: the project's own site server if it has one, otherwise `python3 <skill>/scripts/proposals.py serve --doc <document>`.

   **Stop.** Record the approved structure in NOTES.md (each piece with its number, title, file and diagram flag, and `status: todo`), record the answer to the structure question, write the proposed setup under Questions, and set `waiting_for: "confirmation of the working setup"`. Ask the author to confirm or edit, and end the turn with the menu.

5. **Complete NOTES.md.** If the working folder changed, move NOTES.md and inputs/ there. Set `stage: pieces`, each piece's mode, the default mode, the ledger (with its Roles table when the document describes decisions), the standing rules, the checks and the decisions taken so far as D-n lines (the structure and the rules), and record the answer to the setup question. If a preview server is used, start it in the background and give the address. Then go on as SKILL.md describes under "Working through the pieces".

**Exit:** NOTES.md has `stage: pieces`; every piece has a number, a title, a file, a diagram flag, a mode and `status: todo`; the ledger has its budget and, when the document describes decisions, a Roles table with an owner role for each; the standing rules, the checks and the D-n lines for the structure and the rules are in place; no question from this stage is open.
